1 · What is Generative AI?

Become the language model

For breakfast I like to eat …
She opened the door and saw …

Which continuation is likely, and why?

Interactive activity: LLM Probability Lab

Starting point from topics/01-what-is-generative-ai.md: collect answers, treat their frequency as a rough probability, and sample one continuation. Then move to the LLM Probability Lab. Token and temperature still need age-appropriate explanations. Source: https://llm-probability.org/

2 · Prompt Engineering

A prompt describes the target

Task+ Context+ Constraints+ Output format

Does the result meet our criteria — not: Does the prompt sound clever?

Possible activity from topics/02-prompt-engineering.md: Prompt Olympics. Every group works toward the same target while requirements are added gradually. The existing vibe-coding example may later provide an authentic transfer task, but it still needs privacy and verification questions.

3 · Fabrications

Good writing is not evidence

Identify the claim

What exactly does the answer claim?

Become suspicious

What seems too precise, unfamiliar, or implausible?

Find a source

Which independent source could support it?

Explain the verdict

Supported, false, or not established?

Students investigate fabrications through the Hallucination Treasure Hunt and Fact-Check Race rather than only hearing a warning. Safe example claims and a short source hierarchy still need development. A second AI system is not an independent source.

4 · Online Privacy

What does the AI really need to know?

Okay to share?

A general question without personal details

Think first

School, photos, location, or private experiences

Do not enter

Passwords, addresses, or medical information

Rewrite the request

Remove or anonymize details that are not needed

The Privacy Game from topics/04-online-privacy.md is the starting point. These categories begin a discussion; they are not complete legal guidance. Approved services, accounts, age limits, and the school's privacy policy must be settled before classroom use.

5 · Bias in AI

One result is not an experiment

Change one variable→ Repeat the test→ Count observations→ Draw a careful conclusion

What patterns might come from training data?

Bias Laboratory from topics/05-bias-in-ai.md: use controlled comparison and repeated observations instead of judging one output. Examples and facilitation guidance need review for an age-appropriate, discrimination-aware activity.

6 · AI Relationships & Mental Health

Can say is not the same as can feel

Can a chatbot say it?

“I understand you.”

Can it know it?

Only from text and information available to it.

Can it feel it?

It generates language but does not experience friendship or concern.

Who can really help?

Trusted people and appropriate professional support.

Starting points: the AI Companion Experiment in topics/06-ai-relationships-and-mental-health.md and Cyberlite's Behind the Chat. This topic requires trained colleagues, clear support routes, and sensitive discussion guidelines before it is taught. Source: https://www.cyberlite.org/behind-the-chat
